The Content Record, through its whole life

Mockup #1 tests the claim at the centre of the Creator Hub design: that one Content record — a creator's piece of work carried from raw upload to published performance — can be the place Home, Tasks, edit review, approvals, publishing and intelligence all point at. Revision 2 folds in the product review: approvals with provenance that are separate from deliverable completion, brand feedback relayed through EMG, a second production cycle on the same lineage, contextual publishing, a stable region order, and a per-rung epistemic ladder — all on the same page, with no new screens.

Design only. All names, dates and numbers are examples for judging the design. Content, Version, Deliverable, Performance and the other product nouns are the approved product vocabulary; their authoritative placement is decided in the architecture pass. The record composes campaign, opportunity, work, deliverable, performance and intelligence truth — it owns none of them.

Rules the chain keeps

  • Approvals are marks on a version, with actor, requirement and time; a new version needs its own.
  • A production cycle that has completed stays completed; new feedback starts the next cycle on the same lineage.

  • Brand feedback is never shown as creator feedback or as EMG's own editorial decision.
4 · Evidence access

“View evidence” — four rungs, each with its own standing

Tapping “View evidence” opens Loop's context drawer as a full-screen sheet. The sheet is organised by rung, and each rung states its own basis: a Fact its source and read time; an Observation how Loop derived or read it and how confident it is; an Interpretation what it was inferred from, its sample and its standing; a Recommendation that it is proposed and what it rests on. There is no single “verified / not verified” stamp.

    Interpretation is inferred from evidence and says so: the sample, the split, the standing (proposed by Loop; whether a person at EMG has reviewed it), what would change it, and its limitations — before any recommendation.
  4. 4
    Withheld is a first-class rung: Loop names the question it cannot yet answer and what it needs.
  5. 5
    Proposed recommendations name the rung they rest on and can be dismissed or kept; they never change anything by themselves.
When EMG accepts or rejects the interpretation in the Decision Center, only the standing line changes. The creator's record needs no other update — the same finding, a different standing.

8 · Does it still hold?

What revision 2 shows, and what is left to rule on

Holds

  • Eight states, one page, fixed region order; two new business situations (approval not final; brand feedback via EMG) expressed with no new screen — a checklist, a provenance line and a numbered production.
  • Creative approval, production completion, final approval, publication and deliverable completion are five visibly different moments on the record.
  • Brand feedback is never mistaken for creator feedback or EMG editorial: originator and Loop actor are two facts on the same row, on both seats.
  • The record reads requirements; it never decides them. The two Raw contexts prove the action bar is derived.
  • The four rungs each carry their own basis; “Not yet” survives as a first-class answer.

Left to rule on (specific to this record)

  • Approvals per version. Modelled here as marks on a version: a new version needs every approval again (state 6 resets the list, keeps v2's marks in history). Alternative: an approver's mark carries over unless the change affects them — simpler for the creator, harder to make honest. Recommendation: per version.
  • Pill tone for “Approved by you”. Amber (something still outstanding) rather than green. Confirm.
  • Submitting a raw Original for approvals without an edit. The campaign Raw frame offers only “Request an edit”. If a deliverable can be satisfied by an unedited version, a “Submit for approval” action belongs on the same bar. Not designed until confirmed.
  • Feedback after Final or after publication (a take-down, a re-cut) starts Production 3 on the same lineage; whether it also re-opens the deliverable's requirements is a campaign rule, not a record rule. Out of scope here.
  • The brand's name. “Kona” is shown because it is in the campaign's name; a campaign whose brand is not designated visible would show “the brand”. Confirm the design-pass designation rule applies.
  • “Mark as published” stays manual with a hand-entered post link (design-pass decision 11); detection from a connected platform later.

Not designed here
