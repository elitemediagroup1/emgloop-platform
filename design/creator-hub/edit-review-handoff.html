The edit-review handoff, designed once for both seats

Mockup #2 is the signature workflow: an edit comes back, the creator reviews it against the previous cut, sends timestamped changes or approves, and the editor answers on the same Production. It is designed as one handoff, not two features. The creator side is a review mode over the approved Content Record; the EMG side is the existing Work OS work detail with the fields that handoff needs. Every rule from Mockup #1 is a constraint here, not a variable.

Design only. Names, dates and numbers are examples. Content Record semantics (eight states, per-version approvals, stable regions, provenance, the epistemic ladder, the five separate events) are locked and reused, not redesigned. Backend authorities remain unapproved; nothing here assumes a schema.

  1. 1
    Sheet header. Which version, which record, who returned it and when. Close returns to the record; drafts survive.
  2. 2
    Player with note markers. The segmented control switches versions at the same playhead. Your notes appear as markers on the timeline of the version they were made on; tapping one seeks. The hint under the time names what the other cut shows at this moment.
  3. 3
    “Add a note at 0:02”. Pausing is how you point. The button carries the current time; Compare is the quiet alternative to the segmented control for one-handed use.
  4. 4
    What changed. The editor's creator-visible note first, then Loop's Observation of the difference — tagged as an Observation with its basis, per the epistemic ladder. Never a judgment.
  5. 5
    Your notes (drafts). Each is a timestamp, a sentence and a kind — Change or Keep. A Keep note is an instruction too: it tells the editor what not to touch. Drafts are yours until sent.
  6. 6
    Two decisions. Request changes (with the note count) or Approve. Both open a confirmation that states the consequence in the deliverable's terms. No third state: a question goes through “Add a note” on the record and changes nothing.

Rules the sheets obey

  • A note is a timestamp + a sentence + a kind (Change / Keep); it is a draft until sent, then an instruction with provenance (originated and entered by the creator, refers to that version).
  • Sending never creates a new Production: it reopens stage 1 of the same one. Only feedback after completion starts the next Production (Mockup #1, ruling 4).
  • Approve is per version and says so; the confirmation lists the deliverable's requirements verbatim, so creator approval is never mistaken for finality.
  • For independent content the same confirmation reads “This makes v2 the final. Nothing else is needed.” — derived, not a different flow.
  • “Submit for approval” appears only when the deliverable permits an unedited original; the record reads that, it does not decide it.

Desktop adaptation

  • Review is the record's Compare panel side by side (Mockup #1) with the notes list in the rail and the two decisions in the page head.
  • Notes are markers on the shared scrubber; “Add a note at 0:02” sits inline under it.
  • Request changes and Approve open as drawers on the right; the confirmation content is identical.
  • Nothing exists on desktop that the phone lacks.

Mockup #1 rulings, as applied here

Ruling / invariantWhere it bites in this handoff
Approvals are per VersionThe Approve sheet says “for this version only” and shows the mark landing on v2; a v3 would show the list reset with v2's mark kept in history.
“Approved by you” stays amberAfter approving, the record header and the Approve button state are amber until every requirement is ticked.

Brand naming follows designationSets originated by a brand carry the campaign's creator-visible name or “Brand feedback via EMG”; Sam's replies never expose CRM knowledge.

7 · Left to rule on

Decisions this handoff surfaces

Proposed here — confirm or change

  • Per-note addressed marks with a reply. The editor answers each note at upload; the creator sees “n of m addressed”. Recommendation: keep — it is what makes the second review short.
  • Keep notes. A note kind that says “don't touch this”. Recommendation: keep; it prevents the most common regression.
  • Drafts live on the device until sent. Cheap and private; the cost is that a review started on a phone can't be finished on a desktop. Alternative: creator-private server drafts. Recommendation: device drafts for v1, server drafts when desktop review is common.
  • “Then: save as draft version” for editors — a version the creator can't see yet. Recommendation: keep; without it editors upload to their own machines.

Open

  • Can EMG decline or redirect a change request (out of the deliverable's scope, or a brand constraint)? Proposed: a “Needs a conversation” event that pauses the expected return and posts a creator-visible update. Not designed until confirmed.
  • Approve with notes. Recommendation: no — notes are changes, approval is clean; a “note that isn't a change” goes through Add a note on the record.
  • Reviewing on someone's behalf (a manager, a co-creator). Out of scope for v1, as in the design pass.
  • Range notes (“until 0:06”) — kept minimal; frame capture or drawn regions are later.

Not designed here

Request an Edit (the four-step start of a Production) and the EMG Requests lane are Mockup 3. Home's “Things that need you” and the Person record's creator context are Mockup 4. The Library and Upload are Mockup 5.
